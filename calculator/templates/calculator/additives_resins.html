A) Polymer Processing Aid (PPA)
Function & Properties

Eliminates melt fracture (sharkskin) and reduces die build-up (DLBU), enabling:

  • Higher output rates
  • Lower extrusion temperatures/pressures
  • Improved film clarity and gloss
Mechanism & Substitutes

Primary: Fluoropolymer-based PPA (Dynamar, Viton)

Substitute 1: Silicone-based processing aids

Substitute 2: Metallic stearates (Zinc stearate)

Fluoropolymer migrates to die surface, creating lubricating layer that lowers shear stress.

Concentration Effects
Low Rate (500-800 ppm): Requires "conditioning" time but ultimately eliminates defects
High Rate (>1500 ppm): May increase film haze due to light scattering
Focus Polymers
LLDPE HDPE PP mLLDPE

Essential for high-molecular-weight resins with high shear sensitivity

B) Slip Additive
Function & Properties

Reduces Coefficient of Friction (COF) to prevent:

  • Sticking to processing equipment
  • Blocking during converting
  • Difficulty opening bags
Mechanism & Substitutes

Primary: Erucamide (C22 unsaturated)

Substitute 1: Oleamide (C18 unsaturated)

Substitute 2: Stearamide (C18 saturated)

Fatty acid amides bloom to film surface over time, forming thin lubricating layer. Erucamide provides longer-lasting effect.

Concentration Effects
Low Rate (<800 ppm): High COF, severe blocking issues
High Rate (>2000 ppm): Too slippery, reduced heat seal strength, weak boundary layer
Focus Polymers
LDPE LLDPE PP HDPE

Essential for packaging film handling and conversion processes

C) Anti-Block Additive
Function & Properties

Prevents blocking (film layers sticking together) and improves:

  • Film handling
  • Roll unwinding
  • Processing efficiency
Mechanism & Substitutes

Primary: Synthetic silica (diatomaceous earth)

Substitute 1: Natural talc (magnesium silicate)

Substitute 2: Calcium carbonate (precipitated)

Inorganic minerals create micro-roughness allowing air cushion between film layers. Synthetic silica offers best clarity.

Concentration Effects
Low Rate (<2000 ppm): Poor anti-blocking, film adhesion issues
High Rate (>10000 ppm): Drastic haze increase, reduced clarity, potential tear strength reduction
Focus Polymers
LDPE LLDPE HDPE PP

Critical for high-clarity and thin-gauge packaging applications

D) Barrier Resins - EVOH & Nylon
EVOH (Ethylene Vinyl Alcohol)

Function: Superior oxygen barrier protection

  • Excellent gas barrier properties
  • High clarity and gloss
  • Oil and organic solvent resistance

EVOH Grades:

  • EVOH 32% - Standard barrier
  • EVOH 38% - High barrier
  • EVOH 44% - Ultra high barrier
Nylon (Polyamide) & Substitutes

Primary: Nylon 6 (PA6) - Good oxygen barrier, excellent toughness

Substitute 1: Nylon 66 (PA66) - Higher temperature resistance

Substitute 2: MXD6 Nylon - Enhanced barrier properties

Substitute 3: Amorphous Nylon - Better clarity

Nylon provides good oxygen barrier with excellent mechanical properties and puncture resistance.

Processing Considerations
EVOH: Highly hygroscopic - must be dried to <0.3% moisture
Nylon: Requires drying to <0.1% moisture to prevent degradation
Both: Sensitive to thermal degradation - control melt temperature
Applications
Food Packaging Medical Packaging Pharmaceutical Aroma-sensitive Products

Used in coextruded structures as core barrier layer

E) Tie Layers/Adhesives for Barrier Coextrusion
Function & Properties

Provides strong interlayer adhesion between incompatible polymers:

  • PE/PP to EVOH adhesion
  • PE/PP to Nylon adhesion
  • Critical for multilayer structural integrity
  • Enables complex barrier film structures
Primary Tie Layers & Substitutes

For EVOH:

  • Primary: PE-g-MA (Maleic Anhydride)
  • Substitute 1: EVA-g-MA
  • Substitute 2: Acid Copolymers

For Nylon (Bynel/Admer):

  • Primary: Bynel series (DuPont)
  • Substitute 1: Admer series (Mitsui)
  • Substitute 2: Orevac series (Arkema)
Mechanism & Concentration

Mechanism: Maleic anhydride groups react chemically with OH groups (EVOH) or amine groups (Nylon), forming covalent bonds during coextrusion.

Typical Use: 5-15% in tie layer, 3-8% in total structure
Focus Applications
EVOH Coextrusion Nylon Coextrusion Meat Packaging High-Barrier Films

Essential for multilayer barrier structures in food and medical packaging

F) Ink Components for Flexible Packaging
Adhesion Promoters

Function: Improve ink adhesion to difficult substrates

Primary: Polyurethane-based adhesion promoters

Substitute 1: Chlorinated polyolefins (CPO)

Substitute 2: Titanate coupling agents

Used at 2-5% in ink formulation for treated PE, PP, PET
Extenders/Resins

Function: Modify ink rheology and film properties

Primary: Nitrocellulose resins

Substitute 1: Polyamide resins

Substitute 2: Acrylic resins

Affects ink gloss, flexibility, and resistance properties
Solvents & Diluents

Function: Control viscosity and drying speed

Primary: Ethyl acetate

Substitute 1: Isopropanol (IPA)

Substitute 2: n-Propanol

Solvent balance critical for print quality and drying
Wax & Slip Additives for Inks

Function: Improve rub resistance and surface slip

Primary: Polyethylene wax dispersions

Substitute 1: PTFE wax compounds

Substitute 2: Paraffin wax emulsions

Used at 1-3% to prevent blocking and improve surface properties

Defoamers & Wetting Agents

Function: Eliminate foam and improve substrate wetting

Primary: Silicone-based defoamers

Substitute 1: Mineral oil defoamers

Substitute 2: Acetylenic diol wetting agents

Critical for high-speed printing and uniform ink laydown

G) Antioxidants

Function: Prevent polymer degradation during processing and service life

Primary: Phenolic antioxidants (BHT, Irganox 1010)

Substitute 1: Phosphite antioxidants

Substitute 2: Thioester antioxidants

Typical Use: 500-1500 ppm depending on polymer and application

Function: Protect against UV degradation and color fading

Primary: HALS (Hindered Amine Light Stabilizers)

Substitute 1: UV absorbers (Benzophenones)

Substitute 2: Nickel quenchers

Applications: Outdoor products, UV-sensitive packaging

I) Antistatic Agents

Function: Reduce static electricity buildup

Primary: Glycerol monostearate (GMS)

Substitute 1: Ethoxylated amines

Substitute 2: Quaternary ammonium compounds

Critical for: Dust-sensitive applications, electronic packaging

Function: Control crystallization for improved clarity and stiffness

Primary: Sorbitol-based clarifiers (Millad)

Substitute 1: Sodium benzoate

Substitute 2: Talc (natural nucleator)

Primary Use: Polypropylene for improved clarity and faster cycling

Key Takeaways & Selection Guidelines
Additive Selection Principles
  • EVOH: Choose ethylene content based on barrier requirements and flexibility needs
  • Tie Layers: Match chemistry to specific barrier resin (EVOH vs Nylon)
  • Ink Components: Consider substrate, printing method, and end-use requirements
  • Substitutes: Always test compatibility and performance before full implementation
Critical Processing Notes
  • EVOH/Nylon: MUST be properly dried to prevent degradation
  • Tie Layers: Require adequate layer distribution and contact time
  • Ink Additives: Balance components to avoid conflicts (wax vs adhesion)
  • Barrier Films: Protect EVOH with hydrophobic layers from moisture
Professional Formulation Insight

Successful additive formulation requires understanding both chemical interactions and practical processing constraints. Always conduct trials to optimize additive levels for your specific equipment, materials, and product requirements. Maintain detailed records of formulations and their performance for future reference and troubleshooting.
