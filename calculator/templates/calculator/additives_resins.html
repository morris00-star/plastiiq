Improve extrusion efficiency and film quality

Improve adhesion, strength, and barrier properties

Additives are essential components that modify polymer properties to achieve desired processing characteristics and end-use performance.

A) Polymer Processing Aid (PPA)
Function & Properties

Eliminates melt fracture (sharkskin) and reduces die build-up (DLBU), enabling:

  • Higher output rates
  • Lower extrusion temperatures/pressures
  • Improved film clarity and gloss
Mechanism

Typically a fluoropolymer that migrates to the metal surface of the extruder die, creating a lubricating layer that lowers shear stress at the wall.

Concentration Effects
Low Rate: Requires "conditioning" time but ultimately eliminates defects
High Rate: May increase film haze due to light scattering
Focus Polymers
LLDPE HDPE PP

Used to overcome high shear stresses in high-molecular-weight resins

B) Slip Additive
Function & Properties

Reduces Coefficient of Friction (COF) to prevent:

  • Sticking to processing equipment
  • Blocking during converting
  • Difficulty opening bags
Mechanism

Fatty acid amides (e.g., Erucamide) bloom to the film surface over time, forming a thin lubricating layer.

Concentration Effects
Low Rate: High COF, severe blocking issues
High Rate: Too slippery, reduced heat seal strength, weak boundary layer
Focus Polymers
LDPE LLDPE PP HDPE

Essential for packaging film handling and conversion

C) Anti-Block Additive
Function & Properties

Prevents blocking (film layers sticking together) and improves:

  • Film handling
  • Roll unwinding
  • Processing efficiency
Mechanism

Inorganic minerals (silica, talc) create micro-roughness allowing air cushion between film layers.

Concentration Effects
Low Rate: Poor anti-blocking, film adhesion issues
High Rate: Drastic haze increase, reduced clarity, potential tear strength reduction
Focus Polymers
LDPE LLDPE HDPE PP

Critical for high-clarity and thin-gauge packaging

D/J/K) Tie Layers for Nylon Coextrusion (PE-g-MA and BYNEL)
Function & Properties

Provides strong interlayer adhesion between incompatible polymers:

  • Nylon (PA) to Polyethylene (PE)
  • Critical for structural integrity
  • Enables multilayer barrier films
Mechanism

Maleic anhydride groups react chemically with amine groups on Nylon backbone, forming covalent bonds during coextrusion.

Concentration Effects
Low Rate: Poor adhesion, delamination issues
High Rate: Economically wasteful, potential rheological imbalances
Focus Applications
Nylon Coextrusion Meat Packaging High-Barrier Films

Essential for Nylon/PE multilayer structures

G) Filler

Function: Cost reduction and stiffness modification

Mechanism: Fine solid particles (CaCO₃) dispersed in polymer matrix

High Rate Consequences: Reduced mechanical properties, increased melt viscosity

Key Takeaways
Optimal Concentration Principles
  • Use minimum effective concentration for cost efficiency
  • Balance additive benefits against potential drawbacks
  • Consider interactions between multiple additives
  • Test formulations under actual processing conditions
Common Pitfalls
  • Over-use leading to haze and clarity issues
  • Under-use causing processing problems
  • Incompatible additive combinations
  • Poor dispersion in masterbatch systems
Professional Insight

Successful additive formulation requires understanding both chemical interactions and practical processing constraints. Always conduct trials to optimize additive levels for your specific equipment and product requirements.
