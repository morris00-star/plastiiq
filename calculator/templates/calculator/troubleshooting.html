Extrusion Department

Foundation of quality - Most downstream problems originate here

Key Takeaway for Extrusion Team: Consistent gauge control solves 60% of downstream problems. Implement hourly documentation and SPC monitoring.
Common Error Root Causes Troubleshooting Steps
Gauge Variation
HIGH FREQUENCY
  • Worn/damaged die lips
  • Unstable melt temperature profile
  • Inconsistent screw speed or take-up speed
  • Poor resin mixing (masterbatch distribution)
  • Air ring cooling imbalance (blown film)
  • Human error in parameter setting
  1. Calibrate all sensors (temperature, pressure, speed)
  2. Inspect and clean die lips and air ring weekly
  3. Verify resin blend consistency and feeder calibration
  4. Implement SPC - track gauge every 30 minutes
  5. Standardize startup and shutdown procedures
  6. Train operators on interpreting gauge profiles
Wrong Material/Shade
CRITICAL ERROR
  • Human Error: Wrong resin/masterbatch selection
  • SOP Failure: No pre-production verification
  • Sales Error: Incorrect job card information
  • Contaminated material in hopper/silo
  • Inadequate purging between material changes
  1. Color-code all raw materials and storage areas
  2. Pre-production checklist with supervisor sign-off
  3. First-article inspection before full production run
  4. Material verification at shift changeover
  5. Standardize purging procedure with documentation
Wrinkles & Creases
  • Nip roll pressure imbalance
  • Misaligned collapsing frames
  • Uneven cooling across web width
  • Gauge variation causing web instability
  • Improper tension control
  1. Mechanical alignment check of all guides and rolls
  2. Adjust nip pressures uniformly across width
  3. Review gauge profile and adjust die bolts accordingly
  4. Optimize tension settings for material type
  5. Implement visual inspection every 15 minutes
Pinholes & Black Spots
  • Contaminated resin (dirty regrind, foreign particles)
  • Degraded polymer from overheating
  • Die lip buildup breaking off into film
  • Inadequate filtration
  • Poor housekeeping in production area
  1. Improve raw material handling and storage protocols
  2. Audit regrind quality - implement quality standards
  3. Clean and maintain die area with scheduled downtime
  4. Upgrade filtration system if necessary
  5. Implement 5S in production area
Poor Treatment (Wetting)
  • Dirty or worn treater electrodes
  • Incorrect power setting for material
  • Line speed too high for treatment effectiveness
  • Contamination on film surface
  • Inadequate maintenance schedule
  1. Regular cleaning of treater electrodes (daily)
  2. Monitor and document dyne levels hourly
  3. Correlate line speed with treater power settings
  4. Preventive maintenance schedule for treater system
  5. Train operators on dyne testing procedure
📋 Additional Tips for Extrusion Team
  • Document everything: Keep detailed logs of every parameter change
  • Cross-shift communication: Implement formal handover procedure
  • Preventive maintenance: Don't wait for breakdowns - schedule maintenance
  • Supplier partnership: Work closely with resin suppliers on formulation issues
  • Continuous training: Regular sessions on new materials and techniques

Printing Department

CI Flexo, ST Flexo & Roto - Where brand image is created

Key Takeaway for Printing Team: First-piece approval prevents 80% of print rejects. Never skip the verification process.
Common Error Root Causes Troubleshooting Steps
Misprints & Poor Registration
HIGH FREQUENCY
  • Human Error: Poor plate mounting/cylinder installation
  • Unstable or stretching substrate (extrusion issue)
  • Worn or damaged gears/bearings
  • Incorrect tension control
  • Plate/cylinder damage
  • SOP not followed during setup
  1. SOP for mounting: Verified procedure with checklist
  2. Substrate inspection: Check gauge and treatment before printing
  3. Preventive maintenance: Regular inspection of mechanical components
  4. Tension optimization: Profile for specific materials
  5. Registration marks: Use and verify throughout run
Ink Adhesion & Peeling
CRITICAL ERROR
  • Insufficient substrate treatment (from extrusion)
  • Wrong ink system for substrate (PET vs. PE)
  • Incorrect ink viscosity or dilution ratio
  • Insufficient drying/curing
  • Contamination on substrate surface
  1. Verify treatment: Check dyne level of incoming film
  2. Adhesion test: Standard tape test at startup and hourly
  3. Review ink specs: Ensure compatibility with material
  4. Monitor oven temperatures and airflow regularly
  5. Clean substrate with antistatic equipment if needed
Ink Smudges / Rubbing Off
  • Ink not fully dried/cured
  • Poor ink film hardness
  • Incorrect rewinding (too much tension, too hot)
  • Lack of slip or incorrect COF
  • Ink formulation issues
  1. Optimize drying: Increase temp or reduce line speed
  2. Check ink formulation: Consult with supplier
  3. Adjust rewind tension and ensure cooling
  4. Test rub resistance before full production
  5. Verify slip additive compatibility with ink
Wrong Print / Wrong Direction
  • Human Error: Using wrong plates/cylinders
  • SOP Failure: No verification of artwork vs. job card
  • Sales/Graphics Error: Incorrect job card information
  • Poor communication between shifts
  • Inadequate storage/organization of plates
  1. Digital workflow: Link correct artwork to job digitally
  2. Pre-press proofing: Mandatory sign-off on physical proof
  3. First-sheet approval: Compare to approved proof
  4. Plate storage system: Organized with clear labeling
  5. Cross-check job details with two operators
Color Shade Variation
  • Inconsistent ink viscosity
  • Anilox roll cell volume variation or clogging
  • Variation in substrate color or opacity
  • Doctor blade pressure inconsistency
  • Printing pressure variations
  1. Control viscosity: Use cup and automate if possible
  2. Maintain anilox rolls: Regular cleaning and auditing
  3. Standardize press settings for each job
  4. Color measurement: Use spectrophotometer regularly
  5. Document standards for each job for future reference
📋 Additional Tips for Printing Team
  • Standardized color library: Create approved color standards for repeat jobs
  • Anilox roll management: Implement tracking system for cell volume maintenance
  • Press-side organization: 5S implementation for plates, inks, and tools
  • Regular training: On color theory, press operation, and troubleshooting
  • Supplier collaboration: Work with ink suppliers on formulation improvements

Lamination Department

Creating structural integrity - Failures here mean total product loss

Key Takeaway for Lamination Team: Respect the curing time - 90% of delamination occurs from premature conversion. Never shortcut the process.
Common Error Root Causes Troubleshooting Steps
Delamination
CRITICAL FAILURE
  • Incorrect adhesive mixing ratio
  • Insufficient adhesive application (low coating weight)
  • Inadequate curing time/temperature
  • Poor substrate surface energy (insufficient treatment)
  • Contamination on substrate surface
  • Incompatible materials being laminated
  1. Strict adhesive mixing SOPs: Weight-based, not volume
  2. Monitor coating weight regularly with gravimetric tests
  3. Respect curing schedule: Minimum time before conversion
  4. Audit substrate treatment before lamination
  5. Surface cleaning: Use web cleaners before lamination
  6. Material compatibility testing for new structures
Bubbles in Laminate
  • Trapped air/solvent in adhesive
  • Insufficient nip pressure
  • Contamination on the web
  • Improper unwind/rewind tension
  • Incorrect viscosity of adhesive
  1. Ensure proper drying of adhesive before nipping
  2. Adjust nip roll pressure and temperature
  3. Keep webs clean with air knives and static eliminators
  4. Optimize tension profile throughout the process
  5. Check adhesive viscosity and adjust if necessary
Wrinkles in Laminated Structure
  • Misaligned rollers
  • Uneven tension across web width
  • Gauge variation in substrates
  • Improper web guiding
  • Differential stretch between materials
  1. Check roller alignment regularly
  2. Balance tension across entire web width
  3. Inspect incoming materials for gauge consistency
  4. Calibrate web guides and edge detection
  5. Match material properties when designing structures
📋 Additional Tips for Lamination Team
  • Batch records: Document every batch with parameters and results
  • Adhesive testing: Regular testing of pot life and performance
  • Curing monitoring: Implement system to track curing time for each roll
  • Cross-training: Ensure operators understand adhesive chemistry basics
  • Supplier partnership: Work with adhesive suppliers on optimization

Slitting Department

The final transformation - Errors here directly affect the customer

Key Takeaway for Slitting Team: Blade maintenance is not optional - Dull blades cost more in rejects than replacement blades.
Common Error Root Causes Troubleshooting Steps
Poor Slitting (Burrs, Waves)
  • Dull or damaged slitter blades
  • Incorrect blade overlap/penetration
  • Poor web tension control
  • Misaligned rollers
  • Incorrect blade type for material
  • Worn bearings or mechanical components
  1. Blade maintenance schedule: Replace based on meterage
  2. Precision setup: Use feeler gauges for blade setting
  3. Tension profiling: Optimize for different materials
  4. Roller alignment check during changeovers
  5. Blade selection guide for different materials
  6. Preventive maintenance on mechanical components
Deformed / Dented Cores
  • Human Error: Using damaged cores at startup
  • Excessive winding tension
  • Poor core support during handling/storage
  • Incorrect core size for application
  • Rough handling during transportation
  1. Inspect cores at unloading: Reject damaged cores from supplier
  2. Inspect cores at rewind: Do not mount damaged core
  3. Train staff on proper core handling techniques
  4. Optimize winding tension for core strength
  5. Core specification review for heavy materials
Poor Roll Hardness
  • Inconsistent tension during winding
  • Incorrect taper profile
  • Air entrapment during winding
  • Too much or too little nip pressure
  • Material too slippery or too tacky
  1. Standardize tension settings for each material type
  2. Optimize taper profile based on roll diameter
  3. Use spreader rolls to eliminate air entrapment
  4. Adjust nip pressure according to material characteristics
  5. Test roll hardness regularly with durometer
📋 Additional Tips for Slitting Team
  • Blade inventory management: Keep adequate stock of commonly used blades
  • Setup documentation: Record optimal settings for repeat jobs
  • Roll handling training: Proper techniques to prevent edge damage
  • Quality gates: Inspection points before rolls leave department
  • Communication with previous steps: Feedback loop on material issues

Bag Making Department

Where all previous errors become visible - The final quality checkpoint

Key Takeaway for Bag Making Team: You are the last line of defense - Reject faulty material rather than making faulty bags.
Common Error Root Causes Troubleshooting Steps
Weak Seals / Leaking Bags
HIGH FREQUENCY
  • Extrusion Issue: Incorrect sealant layer or gauge variation
  • Contamination: Ink or slip additive on seal area
  • Incorrect sealing parameters (temp, pressure, time)
  • Worn or dirty seal bars
  • Misaligned sealing jaws
  • Material not designed for application
  1. Verify film structure is correct for application
  2. Conduct seal strength tests regularly (burst test)
  3. Optimize and document sealer settings for each job
  4. Clean and maintain seal bars and Teflon coatings
  5. Check jaw alignment and parallelism
  6. Review material specification with technical team
Bags Tearing (Gussets/Sides)
  • Extrusion Issue: Low tensile/impact strength
  • Design Flaw: Sharp corners on gussets/handles
  • Worn or misaligned cutting dies
  • Incorrect handling during production
  • Material too thin for application
  1. Root cause analysis: Material property or cutting issue?
  2. Review and modify die design if specific point fails
  3. Replace or sharpen cutting blades regularly
  4. Train operators on proper bag handling
  5. Gauge verification of incoming material
Wrong Size / Poor Gusseting
  • Machine misalignment
  • Incorrect machine settings (fold plate, guides)
  • Human Error: Wrong job parameters entered
  • Worn mechanical components
  • Material stretching during processing
  1. First-piece inspection: Measure first 10 bags completely
  2. Standard setup sheets: Checklist for each bag type
  3. Parameter verification: Two-person check for new jobs
  4. Preventive maintenance: Regular check of mechanical wear
  5. Tension control: Minimize stretching during conversion
Incorrect Quantity
  • Human Error: Poor counting or recording
  • Inaccurate bag counter on machine
  • Bags sticking together in count
  • Inconsistent bag length affecting count
  • No verification process
  1. Automate counting: Use and maintain photo-eye counters
  2. Weight verification: Correlate weight with bag count
  3. Double-check by second operator for final packing
  4. Standardize packaging with clear quantity labeling
  5. Regular calibration of counting equipment
📋 Additional Tips for Bag Making Team
  • First/last bag inspection: Check first and last bags of every run
  • Sample retention: Keep samples from each production run
  • Machine capability studies: Understand limitations of each machine
  • Cross-training: Operators should be able to run multiple machines
  • Feedback system: Formal process to report material issues upstream

Sales

The starting point - Errors here waste all production effort

Key Takeaway for Sales Team: A perfect sale has zero surprises - Over-communication prevents 100% of specification errors.
Common Error Root Causes Troubleshooting Steps
Wrong Product Specs on Job Card
CRITICAL ERROR
  • Insufficient Information: From customer
  • Miscommunication or assumptions
  • Lack of technical knowledge in sales team
  • No system for verifying customer requirements
  • Rushing order entry process
  • Language barriers with international customers
  1. Standardized order form: Mandatory fields for all specs
  2. Technical review: All orders reviewed by production manager
  3. Customer approval: Signed approval of mock-up/proof
  4. Sales training: Technical aspects of products
  5. Checklist verification: Before order enters production
  6. Clarification process: For ambiguous requirements
Wrong Material / Print Direction
  • Salesperson selects wrong product from list
  • Ambiguous instructions (e.g., "mirror image")
  • Misinterpretation of customer needs
  • Inadequate product knowledge
  • Copying errors from previous orders
  1. Product code system: Unique, unambiguous codes
  2. Visual aids: Diagrams showing orientation on job card
  3. Standard terminology: Company-wide definitions
  4. Product training: Regular updates for sales team
  5. Order template library: For repeat customers
Incorrect Delivery Timing
  • Over-optimistic production time estimates
  • Failure to check production capacity
  • Not accounting for material lead times
  • Poor communication with production planning
  • Promise dates without verification
  1. Realistic lead times: Based on historical data
  2. Capacity checking: Verify before promising dates
  3. Material availability: Confirm with purchasing
  4. Regular production meetings: Sales and production alignment
  5. Customer communication: Proactive updates on delays
Pricing Errors
  • Incorrect material cost calculations
  • Not accounting for production complexity
  • Outdated pricing information
  • Manual calculation errors
  • Not including all cost components
  1. Automated pricing tools: Reduce manual calculations
  2. Regular cost updates: From purchasing department
  3. Technical review: Complex jobs priced with production input
  4. Checklist pricing: Ensure all cost elements included
  5. Management approval: For non-standard pricing
📋 Additional Tips for Sales Team
  • Factory visits: Sales team should regularly tour production facility
  • Technical glossary: Company-wide definitions of all terms
  • Sample library: Physical samples of all standard products
  • Customer education: Help customers understand specifications
  • Feedback loop: Regular meetings with production about order issues
