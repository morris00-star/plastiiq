Quality Control & Troubleshooting Guide

Defect causes, corrective actions and QC checks for every stage: Extrusion, Printing & Coating, Lamination, Slitting, Bag Making and Sales.

Executive Summary

Critical Issues Identified: Based on complaint analysis, these areas require immediate attention
🚨 High Priority Issues
  • Gauge Variation & Under/Over Size - Most frequent extrusion complaint
  • Misprints, Registration & Re-prints - Leading printing failure
  • Delamination - Critical structural failure (co-extrusion, coating and lamination)
  • Sealing Failure - Poor edge sealing, poor coating balance (OPV) and coated orders
  • Wrong Material/Size/Product - Systemic communication breakdown
  • Unqualified Orders - Missing end-use, machine specs and feasibility review
📊 Impact Analysis
  • Extrusion: 45% of all complaints
  • Printing & Coating: 35% of all complaints
  • Bag Making: 12% of all complaints
  • Sales/Admin: 8% of all complaints

Lamination and slitting errors surface mainly as delamination and seal failures counted under the departments above.

🎯 Recurring Root-Cause Themes
  • Treatment & surface energy: Low dyne affects ink adhesion, coating bond and lamination strength
  • Edge and lane consistency: Edge gauge, sealant and OPV balance determine seal integrity at the customer
  • Job card interpretation: Wrong product, wrong direction and unverified new materials
  • End-use blindness: Food/pharma, filling-line COF and sealing conditions not captured at order stage

Extrusion Department

Foundation of quality - Most downstream problems originate here

Key Takeaway for Extrusion Team: Consistent gauge control solves 60% of downstream problems. Implement hourly documentation and SPC monitoring. Dry hygroscopic resins and check edge quality on barrier and sealable structures.
Common ErrorRoot CausesTroubleshooting Steps
Gauge Variation
HIGH FREQUENCY
  • Worn/damaged die lips
  • Unstable melt temperature profile
  • Inconsistent screw speed or take-up speed
  • Poor resin mixing (masterbatch distribution)
  • Air ring cooling imbalance (blown film)
  • Die bolt / die gap settings not adjusted to the measured profile
  • Human Error Wrong parameter setting or undocumented adjustments
  1. Calibrate all sensors (temperature, pressure, speed)
  2. Inspect and clean die lips and air ring weekly
  3. Verify resin blend consistency and feeder calibration
  4. Implement SPC track gauge every 30 minutes
  5. Rotate/profile-check the web to separate die-related from take-up-related variation
  6. Standardize startup and shutdown procedures
  7. Train operators on interpreting gauge profiles
QC check: Gauge at both edges and centre, start / middle / end of every roll; record in the run sheet.
Wrong Material/Shade
CRITICAL ERROR
  • Human Error Wrong resin/masterbatch selection
  • SOP Failure No pre-production verification
  • Sales Error Incorrect job card information
  • Contaminated material in hopper/silo
  • Inadequate purging between material changes
  • Unlabelled or mixed-up resin bags and masterbatch containers
  1. Color-code all raw materials and storage areas
  2. Pre-production checklist with supervisor sign-off
  3. First-article inspection before full production run
  4. Material verification at shift changeover
  5. Standardize purging procedure with documentation
  6. Retain a shade standard (sample chip/film) for every repeat colour
QC check: Compare first roll against the approved shade standard under the same light source.
Wrinkles & Creases
  • Nip roll pressure imbalance
  • Misaligned collapsing frames
  • Uneven cooling across web width
  • Gauge variation causing web instability
  • Improper tension control
  • Bowed or damaged idler rolls
  1. Mechanical alignment check of all guides and rolls
  2. Adjust nip pressures uniformly across width
  3. Review gauge profile and adjust die bolts accordingly
  4. Optimize tension settings for material type
  5. Implement visual inspection every 15 minutes
Pinholes & Black Spots
  • Contaminated resin (dirty regrind, foreign particles)
  • Degraded polymer from overheating
  • Die lip buildup breaking off into film
  • Inadequate filtration
  • Poor housekeeping in production area
  • Long residence time during stoppages (carbonised polymer)
  1. Improve raw material handling and storage protocols
  2. Audit regrind quality and implement quality standards
  3. Clean and maintain die area with scheduled downtime
  4. Upgrade filtration system if necessary
  5. Reduce temperature/purge before extended stoppages
  6. Implement 5S in production area
Poor Treatment (Wetting)
  • Dirty or worn treater electrodes
  • Incorrect power setting for material
  • Line speed too high for treatment effectiveness
  • Contamination on film surface
  • Excess slip/anti-block additive migrating to the surface (treatment fades)
  • Inadequate maintenance schedule
  1. Regular cleaning of treater electrodes (daily)
  2. Monitor and document dyne levels hourly
  3. Correlate line speed with treater power settings
  4. Re-test dyne on rolls stored more than a few weeks before printing/lamination
  5. Preventive maintenance schedule for treater system
  6. Train operators on dyne testing procedure
QC check: Dyne pen / dyne solution test on the treated side at start, middle and end of each roll.
Under / Over Size (Gauge, Layflat Width & Length)
COMMON ERROR
  • Output rate (kg/hr) not calibrated against line speed for target gauge
  • Incorrect screw RPM or take-up speed ratio set at startup
  • Blow-up ratio (BUR) or layflat width set incorrectly, or drifting during the run
  • Micrometer / gauge instrument out of calibration
  • Job card target gauge or size not verified before full run
  • Material/resin density assumption incorrect for the formula used
  • Edge trim width not allowed for in the job card width
  1. Verify target gauge and size against the job card before setup, every time
  2. Calibrate output rate (kg/hr) against line speed for the specified gauge
  3. Check layflat width and BUR at start, then every roll change
  4. Sample-check gauge at start, middle, and end of every run
  5. Maintain a calibration schedule for micrometers, tapes and gauge instruments
  6. Hold first roll for QC sign-off before continuing production
  7. Weigh a metre sample (g/m) and back-calculate gauge as a cross-check
QC check: Gauge (µm), layflat width (mm) and g/m vs job card tolerance; record on the first-roll sheet.
Die Lines and Streaks Leading to Ruptures
  • Scratched or damaged die lips
  • Contamination lodged in the die gap
  • Degraded polymer buildup at the die exit
  • Dirty or worn breaker plate/screen pack
  • Inconsistent melt flow across the die width
  • Hard gels or burnt particles dragging on the die lip
  1. Inspect and polish die lips on a fixed schedule (use brass/copper tools only)
  2. Clean the die exit during every changeover
  3. Replace screen packs based on contamination level, not just time elapsed
  4. Upgrade melt filtration where streaking recurs
  5. Track die line location across the roll (measure distance from edge) to isolate the root cause
  6. Reject rolls below a defined severity threshold before they reach rupture downstream
  7. Alert printing/lamination when a streaked roll is released on concession
QC check: Hold the roll against light and mark line position; a fixed position on every wrap = die/screen issue.
Delamination in Co-Extrusion
NYLON (PE/Nylon/PE) or EVOH (PE/EVOH/PE) barrier structures
CRITICAL FAILURE
  • Incompatible or insufficient tie-layer (adhesive resin) selection
  • Incorrect layer ratio for the specified structure
  • Moisture in Nylon or EVOH resin (both hygroscopic, require drying)
  • Temperature profile mismatch between layers; tie layer too cold or overheated
  • Poor layer distribution or interfacial instability at the die
  • Tie-layer resin contaminated or past shelf life
  • Nylon/EVOH layer exposed to humidity after extrusion before conversion
  1. Verify tie-layer resin spec and thickness ratio against the approved structure
  2. Mandatory resin drying (dew point check) for Nylon/EVOH before every run
  3. Verify extruder temperature profiles per layer material, not a single blanket setting
  4. Run a layer-distribution check (cross-section under microscope) at startup
  5. Check each extruder output (kg/hr) against the recipe to confirm layer ratios
  6. Cross-check structure against the approved recipe before production begins
  7. Protect rolls from humidity (wrap/seal) after extrusion, especially EVOH
QC check: Hand peel test between layers on first roll and after 24 h; layer thickness by cross-section.
Gel Formation and Un-melts
  • Degraded or cross-linked polymer present in regrind
  • Inadequate melt residence time or barrel temperature
  • Contamination from incompatible resin streams (e.g. PP in PE, or different MFI grades)
  • Poor screw design or excessive screw wear for the resin in use
  • Inadequate filtration
  • Resin grade mismatch in blends (very different melt index)
  1. Audit regrind source and blend ratio
  2. Verify barrel temperature profile is adequate for full melt
  3. Inspect the screw for wear on a maintenance schedule
  4. Upgrade/maintain melt filtration (finer mesh, scheduled screen change)
  5. Segregate incompatible resin streams in storage and handling
  6. Check MFI compatibility of every blend component
  7. Visual gel-count inspection at startup before full production
QC check: Gel count per m² against the customer/internal limit, especially for thin and printed film.
Bubble Instability and Flutter (Blown Film)
NEW / ADDED
  • Air ring cooling imbalance
  • Inconsistent internal bubble pressure (IBC fluctuation)
  • Incorrect or undocumented frost line height
  • Drafts or airflow disturbance in the production bay
  • Inconsistent melt temperature causing bubble wobble
  • Output too high for the cooling capacity of the air ring
  • Low-MI or very high-MI resin behaving differently from the standard recipe
  1. Calibrate and balance air ring cooling
  2. Verify the IBC system is functioning correctly
  3. Monitor and document frost line height for each recipe
  4. Shield the bubble from drafts and cross-winds
  5. Stabilize melt temperature control
  6. Reduce output or increase cooling rather than running at the edge of stability
  7. Train operators to recognize early flutter warning signs
Poor Sealing on the Sides (Customer-Facing)
CRITICAL FAILURE
  • Sealant layer formulation or gauge inconsistent specifically at the film edges
  • Edge trim variation causing uneven sealant distribution
  • Contamination at the film edge from the trimming process
  • Gauge variation concentrated at the edges (bowing)
  • Excess slip/additive bloom at the edges or on the seal layer
  • Customer sealing parameters not matched to the sealant (customer-side)
  1. Verify sealant layer consistency across the full web width, including edges
  2. Inspect the edge trim system for dust/contamination
  3. Check gauge profile specifically at the edges, not just the center web
  4. Run seal strength test (N/15 mm) and hot tack on samples from both edges and centre
  5. Communicate edge-specific seal test results to the bag-making QC team
  6. Coordinate with the customer on sealing temperature/pressure/dwell and share the seal-initiation curve
QC check: Seal strength at edge vs centre; a gap of more than 15–20% points to an edge-specific problem.
📋 Additional Tips for Extrusion Team
  • Document everything: Keep detailed logs of every parameter change
  • Cross-shift communication: Implement formal handover procedure
  • Preventive maintenance: Don't wait for breakdowns - schedule maintenance
  • Supplier partnership: Work closely with resin suppliers on formulation issues
  • Continuous training: Regular sessions on new materials and techniques
  • Barrier structure handling: Dry hygroscopic resins (Nylon, EVOH) and verify tie-layer specs before every barrier run
  • Edge quality matters: Don't check center-web gauge alone - edges determine seal integrity downstream
  • Release status: Tag every roll with treatment (dyne), gauge and gel-level status before it leaves extrusion

Printing Department

CI Flexo, ST Flexo & Roto, including coating (OPV) done on the printing machine - Where brand image is created

Key Takeaway for Printing Team: First-piece approval prevents 80% of print rejects. Never skip the verification process, and treat coating as a controlled process: weight, COF, cure and seal test.
Common ErrorRoot CausesTroubleshooting Steps
🖨 Printing Defects
Misprints & Poor Registration
HIGH FREQUENCY
  • Human Error Poor plate mounting/cylinder installation
  • Unstable or stretching substrate (extrusion issue)
  • Worn or damaged gears/bearings
  • Incorrect tension control
  • Plate/cylinder damage
  • SOP not followed during setup
  1. SOP for mounting Verified procedure with checklist
  2. Substrate inspection Check gauge and treatment before printing
  3. Preventive maintenance Regular inspection of mechanical components
  4. Tension optimization Profile for specific materials
  5. Registration marks Use and verify throughout run
Re-prints & Print on Either Side (Surface vs Reverse Print, Double-Pass Jobs)
  • Wrong side printed surface print done on a reverse-print job, or vice versa (mirror image not applied)
  • Film unwound the wrong way round (treated side facing away from the print unit)
  • Second-pass registration drift when printing the other side or re-running a printed roll
  • Ink set-off / marking from the first print onto rollers or the reverse side
  • Reverse-print layers printed in the wrong order (white/colours reversed)
  • Re-printing over existing ink without checking solvent attack or adhesion of the first print
  • Job card not stating print side, unwind direction or whether the roll is a re-run
  1. Confirm print side (surface / reverse), unwind direction and mirror-image requirement on the job card before mounting plates
  2. Mark the treated side on every roll and verify at unwind
  3. Use a physical proof from the same orientation, signed off, for both sides
  4. For second-pass jobs align registration marks to the first print and check at start and every roll
  5. Check ink compatibility and adhesion of first-pass ink before over-printing
  6. Clean impression rollers and check for set-off between passes
  7. Record re-prints with cause code (wrong side, registration, ink, artwork) for trend analysis
QC check: Compare first piece to the approved proof in the correct reading direction; confirm treated side facing the cylinder.
Ink Adhesion & Peeling
CRITICAL ERROR
  • Insufficient substrate treatment (from extrusion)
  • Wrong ink system for substrate (PET vs. PE vs. Nylon)
  • Incorrect ink viscosity or dilution ratio
  • Insufficient drying/curing
  • Contamination on substrate surface
  • Excess slip additive migrating to the surface
  1. Verify treatment Check dyne level of incoming film
  2. Adhesion test Standard tape test at startup and hourly
  3. Review ink specs Ensure compatibility with material
  4. Monitor oven temperatures and airflow regularly
  5. Clean substrate with antistatic equipment if needed
Ink Smudges / Rubbing Off
  • Ink not fully dried/cured
  • Poor ink film hardness
  • Incorrect rewinding (too much tension, too hot)
  • Lack of slip or incorrect COF
  • Ink formulation issues
  • Retained solvent from insufficient airflow/exhaust
  1. Optimize drying Increase temp or reduce line speed
  2. Check ink formulation Consult with supplier
  3. Adjust rewind tension and ensure cooling
  4. Test rub resistance before full production
  5. Verify slip additive compatibility with ink
Wrong Print / Wrong Direction
  • Human Error Using wrong plates/cylinders
  • SOP Failure No verification of artwork vs. job card
  • Sales/Graphics Error Incorrect job card information
  • Poor communication between shifts
  • Inadequate storage/organization of plates
  1. Digital workflow Link correct artwork to job digitally
  2. Pre-press proofing Mandatory sign-off on physical proof
  3. First-sheet approval Compare to approved proof
  4. Plate storage system Organized with clear labeling
  5. Cross-check job details with two operators
Color Shade Variation
  • Inconsistent ink viscosity
  • Anilox roll cell volume variation or clogging
  • Variation in substrate color or opacity
  • Doctor blade pressure inconsistency
  • Printing pressure variations
  1. Control viscosity Use cup and automate if possible
  2. Maintain anilox rolls Regular cleaning and auditing
  3. Standardize press settings for each job
  4. Color measurement Use spectrophotometer regularly
  5. Document standards for each job for future reference
🧴 Coating Problems (OPV / Coating Applied on the Printing Machine)
Poor Coefficient of Friction (COF) / Slidability
  • Coating weight too low or too high (OPV laydown not controlled)
  • Slip additive in the coating or film not at the required level, or blooming out and wearing off
  • Under-cured coating giving a tacky or high-friction surface
  • Wrong coating type for the end use (e.g. high-slip vs. sealable)
  • COF drifting with storage temperature and time (additive migration)
  • COF specification not matched to the customer's filling machine
  1. Test COF (film-to-film and film-to-metal) at start and every roll against the spec, not only at order approval
  2. Control coating weight with gravimetric checks and anilox volume
  3. Verify cure/drying (oven temp, airflow, speed) before rewinding
  4. Re-test COF after 24–48 h and after storage to catch additive migration
  5. Request the customer's machine COF window before running the job
  6. Adjust slip package with the coating supplier when drift is repeated
QC check: Static and kinetic COF per ASTM D1894 / ISO 8295 vs the specified range.
Optical Defects (Haziness, Ghosting and Creasing)
  • Haziness coating applied too heavy, incompletely dried, or incompatible with the film
  • Haziness moisture or humidity pick-up, or additive bloom
  • Ghosting uneven coating or ink laydown from the anilox/cylinder pattern repeating on the web
  • Ghosting solvent entrapment, poor flow-out or off-contact doctor blade
  • Creasing web tension imbalance or wrinkles entering the nip or oven
  • Creasing uneven film gauge or poor web guiding
  • Dirty anilox, cylinder or oven contaminating the coating surface
  1. Check coating viscosity and flow-out; adjust to the supplier's window
  2. Verify anilox condition and cell volume; clean or replace if clogged or worn
  3. Check oven temperature profile and airflow so coating dries evenly without trapping solvent
  4. Review web tension and guide alignment through the coater, oven and rewind
  5. Inspect incoming film for gauge variation and wrinkles before coating
  6. Control ambient humidity and storage of coating materials
  7. Compare against a retained gloss/haze standard at start and every roll
QC check: Haze (%) and gloss measurement vs standard; visual check against the black/white background and light box.
Odor and Food Contamination
CRITICAL FAILURE
  • Retained solvent in the coating (insufficient drying or exhaust)
  • Coating/ink or additive system not food-contact approved
  • Cross-contamination from other coating or ink lines
  • Poor curing or residual unreacted components migrating
  • Rolls stored in an area with solvent vapours or odours
  • Rewinding the film hot, which traps volatiles
  1. Measure residual solvent (GC headspace) on every coated food/pharma order and keep records
  2. Use only food-grade coating and ink approved for the end use; hold supplier compliance declarations
  3. Verify oven drying and exhaust, and lower line speed if odour is detected
  4. Run an odour (sensory) test on the first roll and a retained sample
  5. Clean the coating unit and anilox properly between jobs; segregate non-food and food runs
  6. Store rolls away from solvents and wrap them promptly
QC check: Residual solvent (mg/m²) vs the customer limit; odour check on a sealed sample after 24 h.
Delamination and Peeling (Bond Failure)
CRITICAL FAILURE
  • Poor adhesion of coating to the film from low surface energy (insufficient treatment)
  • Coating not compatible with the ink or film beneath it
  • Insufficient curing/drying or wrong coating weight
  • Contamination on the surface (dust, slip bloom, oil)
  • Wrong coating chemistry for the structure
  • Aggressive conditions in use (heat, moisture, product contact) that were not tested
  1. Verify dyne level of the substrate before coating
  2. Tape test (adhesion) on every roll at start, middle and end
  3. Check ink-to-coating compatibility with the supplier for new combinations
  4. Control coating weight and cure; keep a log of oven settings per job
  5. Check substrate cleanliness and film storage conditions
  6. Run end-use simulation tests (heat, moisture, product contact) on new structures
QC check: Tape test per ASTM D3359 / internal method and peel strength vs spec.
"Blocking" and Tearing on the Filling Line
  • Coating not fully dried or cured at rewind, so layers stick together
  • Rewind tension or temperature too high; rolls stored hot
  • Coating too soft or tacky for the storage/transport conditions
  • Insufficient anti-block in the coating or film
  • Hot, humid storage; rolls stacked or stored under load
  • Roll hardness too high, leading to blocking and ruptures when unwinding on the filling line
  • Tear initiation from edge damage or defects (e.g. die lines)
  1. Allow full cooling and drying before rewind; check temperature at the rewinder
  2. Lower rewind tension and use a taper profile for coated rolls
  3. Test blocking (unwind force / ASTM D3354) on retained samples after storage conditioning
  4. Review anti-block level with the coating supplier
  5. Store rolls in cool, dry conditions and avoid stacking heavily
  6. Check roll hardness and edge quality before release
  7. Collect filling-line feedback (unwind force, tearing events) and link it to roll numbers
QC check: Unwind retained roll samples after 24–48 h at warm storage conditions; force and surface damage on separation.
Sealing Failure Due to Poor Coating
CRITICAL FAILURE
  • Coating applied on or over the seal area, or incomplete masking/registration of the OPV window
  • Coating weight too high on the sealing side, so sealing temperature is not reached
  • Coating or slip additive migrating onto the sealant surface
  • Incompatible coating with the sealant layer (seals peel instead of fusing)
  • Coating not fully cured, so it smears into the seal
  • OPV pattern not balanced for the sealing jaws (see Slitting)
  1. Check the coating pattern against the job card (seal areas must remain uncoated or use a heat-sealable lacquer)
  2. Control coating weight on the seal side
  3. Run seal strength and hot tack tests on coated samples across the web width
  4. Check cure and migration by testing seals after 24–48 h
  5. Confirm with the customer the sealing temperature, pressure and dwell for the structure
  6. Hold and segregate coated rolls that fail the seal test before slitting
QC check: Seal strength (N/15 mm) at the customer's sealing parameters and across the web width.
📋 Additional Tips for Printing Team
  • Standardized color library: Create approved color standards for repeat jobs
  • Anilox roll management: Implement tracking system for cell volume maintenance
  • Press-side organization: 5S implementation for plates, inks, and tools
  • Regular training: On color theory, press operation, and troubleshooting
  • Supplier collaboration: Work with ink and coating suppliers on formulation improvements
  • Coated orders: Keep a separate checklist (coating weight, COF, residual solvent, seal test) and sign it off before the roll goes to slitting
  • Orientation discipline: Mark print side, treated side and unwind direction on every roll core

Lamination Department

Creating structural integrity - Failures here mean total product loss

Key Takeaway for Lamination Team: Respect the curing time - 90% of delamination occurs from premature conversion. Read the job card, verify every roll and never shortcut the process.
Common ErrorRoot CausesTroubleshooting Steps
Delamination
CRITICAL FAILURE
  • Incorrect adhesive mixing ratio
  • Insufficient adhesive application (low coating weight)
  • Inadequate curing time/temperature
  • Poor substrate surface energy (insufficient treatment)
  • Contamination on substrate surface
  • Incompatible materials being laminated
  • Ink or coating not compatible with the adhesive (reverse-print or OPV over-ink)
  • Slip additive from the film blooming to the bond interface
  1. Strict adhesive mixing SOPs Weight-based, not volume
  2. Monitor coating weight regularly with gravimetric tests
  3. Respect curing schedule Minimum time before conversion
  4. Audit substrate treatment before lamination
  5. Surface cleaning Use web cleaners before lamination
  6. Material compatibility testing for new structures
  7. Test bond strength at 24 h and at the end of cure, and record
Laminating Wrong Products
  • Failure to interpret the job card (structure, layer order, print direction or adhesive type misread)
  • Job card left unread operator relies on the previous job or verbal instructions
  • Lack of film knowledge cannot tell treated from untreated side, PET from BOPP from nylon, or matt from gloss
  • Unlabelled or mislabelled rolls, or similar-looking films stored together
  • No roll-to-job-card verification at unwind
  • Wrong adhesive/hardener for the structure
  • Wrong layer sequence (e.g. print film and sealant film swapped)
  • Pressure to meet delivery without a pre-run check
  1. Pre-run checklist read the job card with a second person; confirm structure, layer order, print direction, adhesive and gauge
  2. Verify every roll at unwind against the job card (label, material ID, gauge, treated side)
  3. Identification of films train operators with sample cards (burn test, dyne, feel, appearance) for the films in use
  4. Label and segregate similar films; use colour-coded roll tags
  5. First-metre inspection with QC sign-off before the full run; keep a retained sample of the laminate
  6. Structure confirmation test the first roll (thickness, burn/solvent test if needed) against the specification
  7. Escalate unclear job cards instead of running on assumptions
QC check: Layer-by-layer check of the finished structure against the job card (thickness, orientation, print direction).
Bubbles in Laminate
  • Trapped air/solvent in adhesive
  • Insufficient nip pressure
  • Contamination on the web
  • Improper unwind/rewind tension
  • Incorrect viscosity of adhesive
  1. Ensure proper drying of adhesive before nipping
  2. Adjust nip roll pressure and temperature
  3. Keep webs clean with air knives and static eliminators
  4. Optimize tension profile throughout the process
  5. Check adhesive viscosity and adjust if necessary
Wrinkles in Laminated Structure
  • Misaligned rollers
  • Uneven tension across web width
  • Gauge variation in substrates
  • Improper web guiding
  • Differential stretch between materials
  1. Check roller alignment regularly
  2. Balance tension across entire web width
  3. Inspect incoming materials for gauge consistency
  4. Calibrate web guides and edge detection
  5. Match material properties when designing structures
Tunneling & Curl
  • Different tension or shrinkage in the two webs
  • Uneven adhesive coating weight
  • Nip pressure not uniform across the width
  • Curing under tight or uneven roll tension
  1. Match tension of both webs at the laminator
  2. Check adhesive coating uniformity across the width
  3. Re-set nip pressure and inspect nip roll condition
  4. Reduce rewind tension on freshly laminated rolls
Residual Solvent / Odor in Laminate
  • Adhesive not fully dried in the oven
  • Wrong oven temperature or airflow for the line speed
  • Solvent-based adhesive retained between layers (dense barrier films)
  • Insufficient curing time before use
  1. Verify oven settings and exhaust for the line speed
  2. Measure residual solvent on food/pharma structures (GC)
  3. Allow adequate curing before release to slitting
📋 Additional Tips for Lamination Team
  • Batch records: Document every batch with parameters and results
  • Adhesive testing: Regular testing of pot life and performance
  • Curing monitoring: Implement system to track curing time for each roll
  • Cross-training: Ensure operators understand adhesive chemistry basics
  • Supplier partnership: Work with adhesive suppliers on optimization
  • Film knowledge: Run regular film-identification training and keep a sample board at the laminator
  • Job card discipline: No run starts until the job card is read, signed and matched to the roll labels

Slitting Department

The final transformation - Errors here directly affect the customer

Key Takeaway for Slitting Team: Blade maintenance is not optional - dull blades cost more in rejects than replacement blades. On coated orders, test every lane for sealing before release.
Common ErrorRoot CausesTroubleshooting Steps
Poor Slitting (Burrs, Waves)
  • Dull or damaged slitter blades
  • Incorrect blade overlap/penetration
  • Poor web tension control
  • Misaligned rollers
  • Incorrect blade type for material
  • Worn bearings or mechanical components
  1. Blade maintenance schedule Replace based on meterage
  2. Precision setup Use feeler gauges for blade setting
  3. Tension profiling Optimize for different materials
  4. Roller alignment check during changeovers
  5. Blade selection guide for different materials
  6. Preventive maintenance on mechanical components
Sealing Failure of Coated Orders Due to Poor Balancing of OPV
  • OPV not balanced across the web width the slit rolls come from areas with different coating weights
  • Edge lanes carrying heavy or light OPV compared with the centre
  • Slit position placing OPV where the customer needs a seal (or cutting through the sealing area)
  • Slit lane layout not matched to the coating/OPV pattern on the printed web
  • Poor coating weight or cure records transferred from the printing department
  • No seal test on each slit lane before release
  • Coating migration onto the seal surface in rolls stored for long periods
  1. Request the coating profile (weight and pattern) from printing before planning the slit layout
  2. Seal-test samples from each lane (edges and centre) on every coated order before the rolls are released
  3. Plan slit lanes so seal areas fall on correctly coated zones; avoid mixing heavy and light OPV in one set of rolls
  4. Hold and segregate lanes that fail the seal test; feed back results to printing to rebalance the coating
  5. Label slit rolls with lane position so failures can be traced to web position
  6. Share seal data with bag-making/the customer, including the sealing parameters used
  7. Review the coating balance at the next print run with the anilox/coating supplier
QC check: Seal strength (N/15 mm) on samples from every lane versus the spec, at the customer's sealing conditions.
Deformed / Dented Cores
  • Human Error Using damaged cores at startup
  • Excessive winding tension
  • Poor core support during handling/storage
  • Incorrect core size for application
  • Rough handling during transportation
  1. Inspect cores at unloading Reject damaged cores from supplier
  2. Inspect cores at rewind Do not mount damaged core
  3. Train staff on proper core handling techniques
  4. Optimize winding tension for core strength
  5. Core specification review for heavy materials
Poor Roll Hardness
  • Inconsistent tension during winding
  • Incorrect taper profile
  • Air entrapment during winding
  • Too much or too little nip pressure
  • Material too slippery or too tacky
  1. Standardize tension settings for each material type
  2. Optimize taper profile based on roll diameter
  3. Use spreader rolls to eliminate air entrapment
  4. Adjust nip pressure according to material characteristics
  5. Test roll hardness regularly with durometer
Wrong Width, Telescoping & Wrong Unwind Direction
  • Slit width set incorrectly or not checked after setup
  • Edge guiding or tension fault during winding
  • Rolls wound with the print or treated side in the wrong direction
  • Job card does not state the unwind direction
  1. Measure width on first roll and each set
  2. Check unwind direction and print orientation against the job card
  3. Correct guiding and tension before running
  4. Label every roll with direction arrows
📋 Additional Tips for Slitting Team
  • Blade inventory management: Keep adequate stock of commonly used blades
  • Setup documentation: Record optimal settings for repeat jobs
  • Roll handling training: Proper techniques to prevent edge damage
  • Quality gates: Inspection points before rolls leave department; seal test on all coated orders
  • Communication with previous steps: Feedback loop on material issues, especially coating balance
  • Traceability: Record the slit lane position on every roll

Bag Making Department

Where all previous errors become visible - The final quality checkpoint

Key Takeaway for Bag Making Team: You are the last line of defense - Reject faulty material rather than making faulty bags.
Common ErrorRoot CausesTroubleshooting Steps
Weak Seals / Leaking Bags
HIGH FREQUENCY
  • Extrusion Issue Incorrect sealant layer or gauge variation
  • Contamination Ink or slip additive on seal area
  • Coating OPV or coating on the seal area, or uneven coating balance
  • Incorrect sealing parameters (temp, pressure, time)
  • Worn or dirty seal bars
  • Misaligned sealing jaws
  • Material not designed for application
  1. Verify film structure is correct for application
  2. Conduct seal strength tests regularly (burst test)
  3. Optimize and document sealer settings for each job
  4. Clean and maintain seal bars and Teflon coatings
  5. Check jaw alignment and parallelism
  6. Review material specification with technical team
Bags Tearing (Gussets/Sides)
  • Extrusion Issue Low tensile/impact strength
  • Design Flaw Sharp corners on gussets/handles
  • Worn or misaligned cutting dies
  • Incorrect handling during production
  • Material too thin for application
  1. Root cause analysis Material property or cutting issue?
  2. Review and modify die design if specific point fails
  3. Replace or sharpen cutting blades regularly
  4. Train operators on proper bag handling
  5. Gauge verification of incoming material
Wrong Size / Poor Gusseting
  • Machine misalignment
  • Incorrect machine settings (fold plate, guides)
  • Human Error Wrong job parameters entered
  • Worn mechanical components
  • Material stretching during processing
  1. First-piece inspection Measure first 10 bags completely
  2. Standard setup sheets Checklist for each bag type
  3. Parameter verification Two-person check for new jobs
  4. Preventive maintenance Regular check of mechanical wear
  5. Tension control Minimize stretching during conversion
Incorrect Quantity
  • Human Error Poor counting or recording
  • Inaccurate bag counter on machine
  • Bags sticking together in count
  • Inconsistent bag length affecting count
  • No verification process
  1. Automate counting Use and maintain photo-eye counters
  2. Weight verification Correlate weight with bag count
  3. Double-check by second operator for final packing
  4. Standardize packaging with clear quantity labeling
  5. Regular calibration of counting equipment
Bags Cut Off-Register / Print Misaligned to Seal
  • Eye-mark sensor misread or dirty
  • Print repeat length variation from printing
  • Film stretching during conversion
  • Incorrect cut length set for the job

  2. Measure print repeat against the job card

Sales Department

The starting point - Errors here waste all production effort

Key Takeaway for Sales Team: A perfect sale has zero surprises - understand the end use and the customer's machine, treat any new material as a new product, and only accept what the plant can deliver.
Common ErrorRoot CausesTroubleshooting Steps
Wrong Product Specs on Job Card
CRITICAL ERROR
  • Insufficient Information From customer
  • Miscommunication or assumptions
  • Lack of technical knowledge in sales team
  • No system for verifying customer requirements
  • Rushing order entry process
  • Language barriers with international customers
  1. Standardized order form Mandatory fields for all specs
  2. Technical review All orders reviewed by production manager
  3. Customer approval Signed approval of mock-up/proof
  4. Sales training Technical aspects of products
  5. Checklist verification Before order enters production
  6. Clarification process For ambiguous requirements
Wrong Material / Print Direction
  • Salesperson selects wrong product from list
  • Ambiguous instructions (e.g., "mirror image")
  • Misinterpretation of customer needs
  • Inadequate product knowledge
  • Copying errors from previous orders
  1. Product code system Unique, unambiguous codes
  2. Visual aids Diagrams showing orientation on job card
  3. Standard terminology Company-wide definitions
  4. Product training Regular updates for sales team
  5. Order template library For repeat customers
Failure to Identify the End-Use Environment (Critical for Food/Pharma)
  • Order taken without asking what the product will contain or where it will be used
  • Not asking about hot-fill, retort, freezing, humidity or product-contact conditions
  • Barrier, migration or food-contact compliance requirements not captured
  • Shelf-life expectations not discussed
  • Sales team assuming the same film suits all products
  • Odor, taint and residual solvent limits not specified
  1. End-use questionnaire mandatory on the order form: product, fill temperature, storage, shelf life, sterilisation, regulatory market
  2. Technical review of end-use by QC/R&D before quoting
  3. Food/pharma flag on the job card triggers extra tests (residual solvent, odour, migration)
  4. Obtain compliance declarations from suppliers for the structure
  5. Document the agreed end use in the signed customer approval
QC check: Customer-signed end-use declaration attached to the job card.
Ignoring the Customer's Machinery Specs (COF Mismatch)
  • Customer's filling or bag-making machine type and speed not recorded
  • COF requirement not asked for or not on the job card
  • Sealing temperature, pressure and dwell of the customer not captured
  • Core size, roll diameter, unwind direction or tension limits ignored
  • Assuming the film that worked before will run on a new machine
  1. Machine data sheet in the order form: machine make, speed, jaw type, sealing parameters, COF range, core and roll specs
  2. Pass the specs to extrusion, printing and slitting through the job card
  3. Offer pilot rolls or trials for new machines before the full order
  4. Record the COF window and test against it at QC
  5. Keep a customer machine profile for repeat orders
QC check: COF (static/kinetic), seal curve and roll dimensions checked against the customer's machine data sheet.
Treating "New Film/Resin" as a Repeat Order
  • Resin, masterbatch, ink, adhesive or coating supplier changed but the order is processed as a repeat
  • Spec changes (gauge, structure, colour) not reflected on a new job card
  • Assuming the previous approval still applies
  • Lack of a change-control process
  • Sales or planning copying the old job card without confirmation
  1. Change-control rule any change in material, supplier, structure or machine makes the order a new product
  2. Trial run and QC approval before the full order is processed
  3. Revalidate key properties (seal, COF, bond, optical) on the new material
  4. Obtain the customer's sign-off on the sample
  5. Update the product master and job card version after approval
QC check: First-run test report compared with the previous approved product for all critical properties.
Accepting Orders Beyond the Plant's Technical Capabilities
  • Structure, gauge, width or print specification outside machine limits
  • Tolerances tighter than the plant can hold
  • Quantity or lead time that cannot be met without compromising quality
  • Orders needing equipment or tests the plant does not have
  • Pressure to win business without technical feasibility review
  1. Feasibility check by production/QC for every new or unusual order, signed before acceptance
  2. Capability matrix of machines (min/max gauge, width, colours, structures) available to sales
  3. Say no or propose alternatives when a requirement cannot be met
  4. Pilot batch before accepting large orders on new capabilities
  5. Review tolerances against process capability data
QC check: Signed feasibility sheet from production/QC before the order is released.
Incorrect Delivery Timing
  • Over-optimistic production time estimates
  • Failure to check production capacity
  • Not accounting for material lead times
  • Poor communication with production planning
  • Promise dates without verification
  • Curing/cooling or testing time ignored
  1. Realistic lead times Based on historical data
  2. Capacity checking Verify before promising dates
  3. Material availability Confirm with purchasing
  4. Regular production meetings Sales and production alignment
  5. Customer communication Proactive updates on delays
Pricing Errors
  • Incorrect material cost calculations
  • Not accounting for production complexity
  • Outdated pricing information
  • Manual calculation errors
  • Not including all cost components
  1. Automated pricing tools Reduce manual calculations
  2. Regular cost updates From purchasing department
  3. Technical review Complex jobs priced with production input
  4. Checklist pricing Ensure all cost elements included
  5. Management approval For non-standard pricing
